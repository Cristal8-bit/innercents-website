Financial Literacy Resources

A curated collection of resources to support your financial literacy journey, from childhood through adulthood.

For Young Learners (Ages 5-11)

Storybooks About Money

Age-appropriate books that introduce money concepts through engaging stories and relatable characters.

Why helpful: Makes learning fun and relatable

Examples: "Alexander, Who Used to Be Rich Last Sunday" and "A Chair for My Mother" teach saving and decision-making through emotional storytelling.

Visual Saving Tools

Clear jars, piggy banks, and visual charts that let children see their money grow in real-time.

Why helpful: Builds concrete understanding

Watching money accumulate visually reinforces saving habits and creates excitement about financial goals.

Interactive Money Games

Educational games that teach counting, sorting, and basic money management through play.

Why helpful: Learning through play

Games like store simulations and coin sorting activities make abstract concepts tangible and enjoyable.

For Teens (Ages 12-18)

Budgeting Apps for Youth

Teen-friendly apps like Greenlight, GoHenry, and FamZoo that teach money management with parental oversight.

Why helpful: Real-world practice in safe environment

These apps provide hands-on experience with digital banking, spending tracking, and saving goals while building financial independence.

First Job Resources

Guides on earning, taxes, paychecks, and workplace financial literacy for teens entering the workforce.

Why helpful: Practical preparation

Understanding W-4 forms, tax deductions, and net vs. gross pay empowers teens to navigate their first employment experiences.

College Financial Planning

Resources on FAFSA, scholarships, student loans, and understanding the true cost of college.

Why helpful: Reduces anxiety and debt

Early planning and understanding of college costs helps families make informed decisions and avoid overwhelming debt.

For Young Adults (Ages 18-30)

Credit Building Resources

Educational materials on credit scores, credit reports, and responsible credit card use.

Why helpful: Builds financial foundation

Understanding credit early prevents costly mistakes and opens doors to better interest rates and financial opportunities.

Investment Platforms

Beginner-friendly investment apps like Robinhood, Acorns, and Betterment that make investing accessible.

Why helpful: Starts wealth building early

Low barriers to entry and educational resources help young adults start investing with small amounts and build long-term wealth.

Renting & Housing Guides

Resources on leases, tenant rights, budgeting for housing costs, and planning for homeownership.

Why helpful: Protects and prepares

Understanding housing costs and rights helps young adults avoid scams, budget effectively, and work toward future goals.

Financial Wellness & Mental Health

Money & Emotions Resources

Books, podcasts, and workshops exploring the psychological aspects of money and financial behavior.

Why helpful: Addresses root causes

Understanding the emotional drivers behind spending helps create lasting behavioral change and reduces financial stress.

Financial Counseling Services

Non-profit organizations offering free or low-cost financial counseling and debt management support.

Why helpful: Professional guidance

Organizations like NFCC and local community centers provide personalized support for those facing financial challenges.

Community Financial Programs

Local initiatives, credit unions, and community banks that offer financial education and accessible services.

Why helpful: Community-centered support

Community-based programs understand local contexts and provide culturally relevant financial education and services.

Digital Tools & Technology

Budgeting Tools

Apps and software like Mint, YNAB (You Need A Budget), and EveryDollar for tracking income and expenses.

Why helpful: Real-time awareness

Automated tracking removes guesswork and provides instant visibility into spending patterns and financial health.

Financial Security Tools

Password managers, identity theft protection, and secure banking practices for the digital age.

Why helpful: Protects your assets

Protecting digital financial information is crucial in an increasingly online financial world.

Financial Education Podcasts

Engaging podcasts covering personal finance, investing, and economic justice for various audiences.

Why helpful: Learning on the go

Podcasts make financial education accessible during commutes, workouts, or daily routines, fitting learning into busy lives.
